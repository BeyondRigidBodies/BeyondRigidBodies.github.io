Overview

Simulation, beyond rigid bodies.

This full-day workshop follows one path from programmable physics to research questions, solver choices, and policy evaluation.

An NVIDIA Warp introduction and interactive demonstration establish a shared contact-and-manipulation example. The Taiwan-led Beyond Rigid Bodies organizing team then leads research clinics and group exercises on non-rigid fidelity, differentiability, Real2Sim2Real, and world models, with contributions from four invited speakers. Participants turn those lessons into explicit modeling assumptions and validation requirements. Closing Newton and Isaac Lab sessions use these requirements to examine multi-solver interfaces and robot-learning workflows through demonstrations, comparisons, and group review.

Academic and industry participants jointly produce a task-to-solver decision record, an evaluation checklist, and prioritized open questions. The program welcomes alternative engines, critical comparisons, and negative results; software demonstrations provide concrete evidence for discussion throughout the day. The core activities remain accessible without a GPU or internet connection.

Four pillars

The technical agenda

i

High-Fidelity Non-Rigid Simulation

FEM, MPM, IPC, frictional contact, compliant materials, cloth, and granular media expose behavior absent from a simple rigid-contact baseline. Participants identify the physical effect that changes task success, the cost of representing it, and a diagnostic scene that tests whether increased fidelity helps.

ii

Differentiable Simulation for Policy Learning

System identification, inverse design, morphology optimization, and policy learning require different derivatives and tolerances for approximation. Participants examine contact discontinuities, stiffness, and long-horizon sensitivity, then choose a gradient check or alternative optimization strategy.

iii

Real2Sim2Real & Digital Twins

Reconstruction, neural scene representations, video-based system identification, and policy evaluation connect observations to a usable physical model. Participants list identifiable parameters, unresolved ambiguities, and held-out observations needed to validate a twin.

iv

Simulation as a World Model

Learned dynamics, neural constitutive models, and structured predictive models can complement physical solvers. Participants ask which predictions a controller needs, how to test consistency over time, and what failures reveal limits to generalization.

Provisional schedule · Updated September 23, 2026

One full day · 09:00–17:30

All times are local to Kuala Lumpur (UTC+8). The full-day format is confirmed. December 3 or 4, 2026 is under consideration; the final date, room, session times, and speaker delivery arrangements remain subject to organizer and workshop-chair confirmation.

The proposed day includes seven hours of programmed activity, one hour for lunch, and two 15-minute breaks. Research clinic labels below describe proposed themes, rather than finalized talk titles.

  1. 09:00–09:10

    Joint welcome & audience poll

    Organizing teams · Introduce the shared manipulation brief and record questions to revisit at the close.

  2. 09:10–10:00

    NVIDIA Warp introduction & interactive demonstration

    NVIDIA team · Predict a parameter change, inspect the result, and document the baseline’s limits.

  3. 10:00–10:15

    Break

    Informal questions and case collection.

  4. 10:15–10:45

    BRB research clinic: reconstruction & digital twins

    Proposed contribution: Srinath Sridhar · Identify observations, unknown parameters, and a calibration check.

  5. 10:45–11:15

    BRB research clinic: structured world models

    Proposed contribution: Yunzhu Li · Define a useful prediction and a test of generalization.

  6. 11:15–11:45

    BRB research clinic: contact-rich simulation & control

    Proposed contribution: Taku Komura · Identify a missing physical effect and a diagnostic scene.

  7. 11:45–12:15

    BRB research clinic: differentiable simulation & design

    Proposed contribution: Tao Du · Examine gradient assumptions and specify a check or alternative.

  8. 12:15–12:30

    Cross-clinic discussion

    BRB moderation and available invited speakers · Consolidate questions for group case work and capture morning guest input.

  9. 12:30–13:30

    Lunch

    Informal exchange.

  10. 13:30–14:30

    BRB facilitated workshop: participant cases & working groups

    BRB team · A 10-minute case lightning round, 40-minute group exercise across the four themes, and 10-minute preparation of evidence and proposed tests.

  11. 14:30–15:00

    Group report-back & joint technical discussion

    BRB moderation with the NVIDIA team · Four 5-minute theme reports and 10-minute prioritization; carry requirements into the Newton session.

  12. 15:00–15:15

    Break

    Transition to practical examples.

  13. 15:15–16:15

    Newton: solver choices & validation

    NVIDIA team · Map requirements, explore a guided example, and critique solver choices to produce a decision record.

  14. 16:15–17:15

    Isaac Lab: from simulation to robot learning

    NVIDIA team · Walk through a task, examine a policy rollout and perturbation, and develop an evaluation checklist.

  15. 17:15–17:30

    Joint synthesis & community follow-up

    Organizing teams and participants · Revisit initial questions, finalize checks and open problems, and identify volunteer follow-up owners.

Each proposed 30-minute research clinic combines 15 minutes of technical input, 10 minutes of participant work or discussion, and 5 minutes of synthesis.
