Overview

Simulation, beyond rigid bodies.

The intersection of computer graphics and robotics has seen immense progress, yet most scalable Embodied AI still relies on rigid-body dynamics. As robots increasingly interact with soft tissue, granular media, deformable objects, and high-precision frictional contact, there is a critical need for next-generation physical simulators that are simultaneously faithful, fast, differentiable, and deployable.

Beyond Rigid Bodies × Newton for Robotics (BRBN) brings the BRB and NVIDIA/Newton teams together for one full-day program. An NVIDIA Warp introduction establishes a shared manipulation example. Four BRB research clinics and participant working groups turn research questions into modeling and validation requirements. Closing Newton and Isaac Lab sessions connect those requirements to solver choices and robot-learning evaluation.

Bring a research question, a work-in-progress case, or a negative result. Participants will develop a task-to-solver decision record, an evaluation checklist, and shared open questions. No installed software or participant GPU is required; alternative engines and critical comparisons are welcome.

Four pillars

The technical agenda

i FEM / IPC contact

High-Fidelity Non-Rigid Simulation

FEM, MPM, and IPC solvers; frictional contact; near-incompressible and hyperelastic materials; granular and elastoplastic media; cloth and thin shells; GPU-accelerated, real-time solvers.

ii ∇θ contact

Differentiable Simulation for Policy Learning

Analytic gradients through contact for system identification, inverse design, computational design and fabrication, and robot manipulation and control.

iii Real → Sim

Real2Sim2Real & Digital Twins

Neural radiance fields and Gaussian splatting; scene and dynamics reconstruction; Sim-to-Real transfer; calibrated, deployable closed-loop pipelines.

iv predict + plan

Simulation as a World Model

Neural and generative world models; learned physics surrogates; neural PDEs/ODEs and reduced-order modeling; multi-modal predictive models for embodied agents.

Invited & presenting speakers

Research clinics meet practical simulation

Four confirmed invited speakers contribute to the BRB research clinics. Clinic assignments and individual times remain provisional; delivery arrangements for Srinath Sridhar and Yunzhu Li are pending.

Invited speaker

Taku Komura

Professor · The University of Hong Kong

character animation · physics-based simulation · contact solvers

Leads research in character animation, physics-based simulation, and interactive techniques. Recent work on whole-body manipulation, contact-rich scene interaction, and GPU-accelerated contact solvers sits at the boundary of animation and robot learning.

Invited speaker

Tao Du

Assistant Professor · Tsinghua University (IIIS)

differentiable simulation · soft robots · inverse design

Develops computational design frameworks and differentiable simulation tools for soft robots, actuated mechanisms, and fabrication-aware optimization, discovering robot morphologies and control policies directly from simulation.

Invited speaker

Yunzhu Li

Assistant Professor · Columbia University (RoboPIL)

world models · deformable manipulation · Real2Sim

Works on structured world models and physics-inspired predictive models for Embodied AI, with emphasis on multi-modal perception, manipulation of deformable objects, and bridging learned simulators with real-world deployment.

Invited speaker

Srinath Sridhar

Assistant Professor · Brown University (IVL)

3D/4D vision · hand–object · dynamic neural fields

Builds foundational methods for 3D/4D understanding of objects, humans in motion, and hand–object interaction. Work on dynamic neural fields, articulated-object understanding, and visuo-haptic pose tracking connects to Real2Sim and digital twins.

Provisional schedule · Updated September 23, 2026

One full day · 09:00–17:30

All times are local to Kuala Lumpur (UTC+8). The full-day format is confirmed. December 3 or 4, 2026 is under consideration; the final date, room, session times, and speaker delivery arrangements remain subject to organizer and workshop-chair confirmation.

The proposed day includes seven hours of programmed activity, one hour for lunch, and two 15-minute breaks. Research clinic labels below describe proposed themes, rather than finalized talk titles.

  1. 09:00–09:10

    Joint welcome & audience poll

    Organizing teams · Introduce the shared manipulation brief and record questions to revisit at the close.

  2. 09:10–10:00

    NVIDIA Warp introduction & interactive demonstration

    NVIDIA team · Predict a parameter change, inspect the result, and document the baseline’s limits.

  3. 10:00–10:15

    Break

    Informal questions and case collection.

  4. 10:15–10:45

    BRB research clinic: reconstruction & digital twins

    Proposed contribution: Srinath Sridhar · Identify observations, unknown parameters, and a calibration check.

  5. 10:45–11:15

    BRB research clinic: structured world models

    Proposed contribution: Yunzhu Li · Define a useful prediction and a test of generalization.

  6. 11:15–11:45

    BRB research clinic: contact-rich simulation & control

    Proposed contribution: Taku Komura · Identify a missing physical effect and a diagnostic scene.

  7. 11:45–12:15

    BRB research clinic: differentiable simulation & design

    Proposed contribution: Tao Du · Examine gradient assumptions and specify a check or alternative.

  8. 12:15–12:30

    Cross-clinic discussion

    BRB moderation and available invited speakers · Consolidate questions for group case work and capture morning guest input.

  9. 12:30–13:30

    Lunch

    Informal exchange.

  10. 13:30–14:30

    BRB facilitated workshop: participant cases & working groups

    BRB team · A 10-minute case lightning round, 40-minute group exercise across the four themes, and 10-minute preparation of evidence and proposed tests.

  11. 14:30–15:00

    Group report-back & joint technical discussion

    BRB moderation with the NVIDIA team · Four 5-minute theme reports and 10-minute prioritization; carry requirements into the Newton session.

  12. 15:00–15:15

    Break

    Transition to practical examples.

  13. 15:15–16:15

    Newton: solver choices & validation

    NVIDIA team · Map requirements, explore a guided example, and critique solver choices to produce a decision record.

  14. 16:15–17:15

    Isaac Lab: from simulation to robot learning

    NVIDIA team · Walk through a task, examine a policy rollout and perturbation, and develop an evaluation checklist.

  15. 17:15–17:30

    Joint synthesis & community follow-up

    Organizing teams and participants · Revisit initial questions, finalize checks and open problems, and identify volunteer follow-up owners.

Each proposed 30-minute research clinic combines 15 minutes of technical input, 10 minutes of participant work or discussion, and 5 minutes of synthesis.
